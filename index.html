I study how formally neutral workplace systems reproduce inequality.

I am a PhD candidate in Sociology at the University of Wisconsin-Madison. My research examines organizations, gender, and work-family policy, with a focus on how workers and employers interpret family leave and how AI reshapes hiring. I combine preregistered experiments, legal case analysis, and computational text methods.

My dissertation, Navigating Family Leave Usage in the Workplace, is supported by an ASA Doctoral Dissertation Research Improvement Grant funded by the National Science Foundation.

Research

Job market paper·Preregistered experiment·N = 2,348

What They Do and Who Is Watching: Peer Signals, Leave Duration, and Parental Leave Culture

Workers hold parental leave rights they do not fully use. Drawing on legal consciousness theory, this preregistered factorial vignette experiment varies the gender, hierarchical role, and leave duration of a peer leave-taker while holding formal policy constant. Hierarchical position produced no consistent main effects. The dominant cultural signal was behavior itself. Twelve-week peer leaves shifted leave intentions and all four cultural perception measures, with effects surviving multiple-testing correction.

Legal case analysis·212 adjudicated disputes·2004–2025

Gendered Organizational Accounts in Family-Leave Litigation

How do employers justify adverse actions against workers who take family leave? This study analyzes adjudicated family-leave disputes and arbitration awards from California, New York, and New Jersey, developing a typology of the administrative, evaluative, and temporal accounts organizations deploy and showing how their use differs by worker gender.

Computational audit·With Eunsil Oh·Under review

The Organizational Embedding of Algorithmic Bias: Evidence from Large-Scale AI Experiments

Large language models increasingly screen job candidates. This study audits how organizational context shapes race and gender discrimination in AI resume evaluation, testing when algorithmic hiring tools reproduce the inequalities of the settings in which they are deployed.

Vignette experiment·With Allison Daminger and Jaclyn Wong·Under review

The Gendered (De)Valuation of Housework: An Experimental Vignette Study of Household Task Perceptions

An experimental study of how the gender of the person performing household tasks shapes perceptions of the work's value, supported by a Time-Sharing Experiments for the Social Sciences (TESS) grant.
